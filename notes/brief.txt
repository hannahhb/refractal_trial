 REFRACTAL



 Dataset quality and
 monitor labeling
 Take-home assignment: 4 hours


 Context
 At Refractal, we build monitors that assess an AI agent's proposed actions before execution. A
 monitor returns Accept or Reject: allow the action to proceed, or block it.
 We have collected agent trajectories from software and operational tasks in sandboxed
 environments. We want to turn these trajectories into useful training and evaluation data for a
 monitor.
 We'd like you to explore the data, follow promising leads, and work out how to turn it into labels we
 can trust. Show us what you discover and how it changes what you would do next.

 The dataset
 58 agent trajectories. Each record contains:
  • messages: the recorded conversation: assistant turns, tool calls, and tool responses.
  • actions: recorded tool events, with stable action IDs, arguments, results, and links to messages
    where available.
  • main_tasks: legitimate task definitions, where available.
  • adversarial_tasks: adversarial objectives, where available.
  • environment, tool_registrations, actor_model, and actor_seed: context about the environment,
    available tools, and agent.
 The supplied records have no Accept/Reject annotations. Treat the task descriptions as generation
 context and investigate what the recorded behavior supports.
 The accompanying ZIP contains the trajectories, field documentation, and a Python loader. Extract it
 before you begin. Python's standard library is enough to load the data.
 After extracting the ZIP, load the data using Python's standard library:
   import gzip
   import json

   with gzip.open("data/trajectories.jsonl.gz", "rt", encoding="utf-8") as f:
       traces = [json.loads(line) for line in f]

   assert len(traces) == 58

 See README.md in the ZIP for field definitions and loading instructions.

 Resources
  • You may use coding agents and other tools you already have access to. We do not provide
    coding-agent subscriptions or API keys for coding agents. Explain which tools you used and how.
  • For model calls in labeling and validation, we provide a Google Vertex AI API key upon request.
    Contact the person who sent you this assignment to arrange access. Record model usage and
    estimated cost where applicable.
  • Build whatever tooling helps. There's no restriction on approach; the length of the traces and the
    reliability of the resulting labels are the constraints to design around.
  • This is an offline data exercise. Read commands in the traces as data; any environment replay
    would need a separate sandbox.

 What we're evaluating
  • Independent judgment: how you frame the problem, prioritize the important uncertainties, test
    assumptions, and revise your approach when the evidence warrants it.
  • Evidence and calibration: findings and labels that are cited, quantified, and clear about
    uncertainty, alongside a sensible way to establish whether your judging method works.
  • Engineering and scale: a working, reproducible prototype and a practical path from this sample
    to an automated labeling pipeline.
  • Communication: clear explanations of your choices, limitations, results, and next steps.
 We value a useful starting point, justified choices, and evidence that helps you improve your
 approach. You can conclude that the dataset needs substantial changes before it serves the intended
 purpose.

 Deliverable
 Two things:
  • Part 1: report. A written report of your findings and proposed labeling approach, with evidence. A
    concise, cited finding beats a pile of hunches.
  • Part 2: pipeline. Runnable code, a sample of its output, instructions to reproduce it, and an
    overview of the pipeline and validation study. Distinguish what you implemented, what you
    measured, and what you propose.
 Write the report for a human reader: explain what you did, what you found, and why it matters. We
 will reject vague, generic filler and claims you cannot explain.
 Feel free to write the report with a coding agent. We recommend giving it detailed instructions, then
 reading the result carefully and checking that you understand every claim, method, and term.
 Anything in the report may come up in the follow-up interview, and you will have the report available
 throughout. Treat the interview as a discussion of your work: if you understand what you submitted
 and can explain your choices, you are well prepared.

 Logistics
  • Time limit: 4 hours. Your four-hour window begins when you submit the start form by pressing
    Start. The confirmation page provides the brief and dataset downloads. Email your report and code,
    or a private link to them, to samuel@refractal-ai.com within that window. We expect a prioritized
    investigation, a working prototype, and a validation plan. Decide how to use the time and explain
    your priorities.
  • Stop at the time limit and tell us what you left unfinished. We will discuss your reasoning and code
    in a 30 to 45 minute walkthrough.
  • If anything seems broken or unclear about the data, access, or the task, contact the person who
    sent you this assignment. Arrange any requested Vertex API access before you press Start. If a
    technical problem interrupts your trial, contact us and describe it in your submission notes.

 Privacy
 Please keep this work trial and its data private. Please don't replicate or redistribute the work trial,
 and delete the supplied datasets and any copies you made after the follow-up interview. Much
 appreciated.

 Final words
 Work trials can feel scary. Work trials should resemble the job; so they should be fun too! We like
 working with people who are curious and enjoy figuring things out together, and we'd like you to get a
 feel for that here.
 We've tucked a small easter egg into the data. Include your best guess in your report; this part is just
 for fun. Follow what catches your attention, ask questions, and tell us what you find. We hope you
 enjoy it.

 The task
 Make reasonable decisions where details are missing, including defining a security policy if your
 approach needs one. Explain what you chose and why. We want to see whether you can get a useful
 approach working, justify your decisions, and identify how to improve it.

 Part 1: What is happening in these trajectories?
 Explore the supplied trajectories. What do you notice? What patterns do you see? How do the attacks
 work? How would you describe and name the attack patterns you find?
 Follow the leads you find interesting, and explain what you inspected and why. Assess the dataset's
 quality and usefulness for training and evaluating an Accept/Reject monitor, and give us evidence.
 We're interested in:
  • Dataset description and threat models: what behaviors are represented, what the attackers
    control and try to achieve, and which monitoring challenges the trajectories could test.
  • Quality and coverage: important problems, gaps, ambiguities, or artifacts in the data, and how
    they affect its use. Explain what you would keep, fix, exclude, or investigate further.
 For every substantive finding, cite the trajectories and messages or action IDs it rests on, and
 quantify it where you can. Be explicit about what you're unsure of and what additional evidence
 would resolve it.

 Part 2: How would you train a monitor to catch these attacks?
 How would you label this data so we can train a monitor to defend against the attacks you identified?
 The monitor should predict Accept or Reject at runtime. It receives the legitimate task, the
 interaction history available so far, and a proposed tool call. It decides before that call executes.
 Devise a labeling pipeline for this use and implement a working prototype. Decide what to label, what
 the labels mean, and how to judge them. Produce structured output and preserve enough evidence
 for someone else to review the decisions. Work through concrete examples.
 Use your pipeline to label all trajectories or a subset of them. Explain what you covered and why.
 Red team your labeling. Devise a study to decide whether the labels are reliable. Run one useful
 check or comparison within the time available, and describe the rest of the study. What does your
 approach do well, where might it fail, and what are its caveats? Explain what you prioritized when
 choosing your pipeline, which metrics guided your decisions, and why. Show one assumption you
 tested and whether the result changed your approach. What would you change with another day,
 three days, or a week?
 Scaling. The trajectories are long, the next corpus may contain 100,000 of them, and labeling can be
 expensive. Describe how your pipeline reads, processes, and reviews data at that scale, including how
 someone could reproduce or resume a run.
 Concrete next step. Finish with the next action you would take and which uncertainty it would
 resolve. Include the next data you would obtain or generate, where it would come from, and why.
